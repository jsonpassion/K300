# Level 2 · Elementary · Book 006 — Feelings and personality

- 기분 | mood, feeling | gibun | 기분이 좋다 = in a good mood; 기분이 나쁘다 often means offended | 오늘은 날씨가 좋아서 기분이 좋아요. | The weather is nice today, so I'm in a good mood.
- 화나다 | to get angry | hwanada | Describes the state; 화내다 is showing it to someone | 친구가 약속을 잊어서 정말 화났어요. | I got really angry because my friend forgot our plans.
- 화 | anger | hwa | 화가 나다 (get angry) vs 화를 내다 (lose your temper at someone) | 동생이 제 옷을 입어서 화가 났어요. | I got mad because my little brother wore my clothes.
- 괴롭다 | to be painful, to be distressing | goeropda | Inner suffering; 괴롭히다 = to bully, to bother someone | 매일 밤 잠을 못 자서 너무 괴로워요. | It's really hard on me because I can't sleep every night.
- 외롭다 | to be lonely | oeropda | ㅂ→우: 외로워요. Noun form: 외로움 (loneliness) | 혼자 외국에 살아서 가끔 외로워요. | I live abroad alone, so I sometimes feel lonely.
- 심심하다 | to be bored, to have nothing to do | simsimhada | Bored from nothing to do; 지루하다 = something itself is dull | 주말에 할 일이 없어서 심심해요. | I have nothing to do this weekend, so I'm bored.
- 지루하다 | to be boring, to be dull | jiruhada | Describes the thing: 영화가 지루하다. Compare 심심하다 (I'm idle) | 수업이 너무 길어서 조금 지루했어요. | The class was so long that it got a bit boring.
- 놀라다 | to be surprised, to be startled | nollada | Takes 에: 소리에 놀라다. 놀랍다 = to be surprising | 갑자기 큰 소리가 나서 깜짝 놀랐어요. | I was startled by a sudden loud noise.
- 걱정하다 | to worry | geokjeonghada | Takes 을/를 or 에 대해: 시험을 걱정하다. Comfort: 걱정하지 마세요 | 엄마는 항상 제 건강을 걱정하세요. | My mom always worries about my health.
- 걱정 | worry, concern | geokjeong | 걱정이 많다 = to worry a lot; 걱정 마 = don't worry (casual) | 시험 때문에 요즘 걱정이 많아요. | I have a lot of worries these days because of exams.
- 걱정되다 | to be worried, to feel anxious | geokjeongdoeda | Subject is the worry: 시험이 걱정돼요 (the exam worries me) | 비가 많이 와서 여행이 걱정돼요. | It's raining a lot, so I'm worried about the trip.
- 긴장하다 | to be nervous, to be tense | ginjanghada | 緊張: tension. 긴장되다 = to feel nervous (state) | 면접 전에 너무 긴장해서 손이 떨렸어요. | I was so nervous before the interview that my hands shook.
- 부끄럽다 | to be shy, to be ashamed | bukkeureopda | Shy or guilty-ashamed; 창피하다 = embarrassed in front of others | 사람들 앞에서 노래하는 게 부끄러워요. | I feel shy singing in front of people.
- 창피하다 | to be embarrassed | changpihada | Public embarrassment: tripping or a wrong answer in class | 길에서 넘어져서 정말 창피했어요. | I fell down in the street and was so embarrassed.
- 부럽다 | to be envious, to envy | bureopda | The envied thing is the subject: 친구가 부러워요. ㅂ→우 | 영어를 잘하는 친구가 너무 부러워요. | I really envy my friend who speaks English well.
- 섭섭하다 | to feel hurt, to be sad to part | seopseopada | Pronounced 섭써파다. Hurt by someone close, or sad at a farewell | 친구가 제 생일을 잊어서 섭섭했어요. | I felt hurt that my friend forgot my birthday.
- 서운하다 | to feel let down, to be sorry to part | seounhada | Near-synonym of 섭섭하다; very common in conversation | 동생이 인사도 없이 떠나서 서운했어요. | I felt let down when my brother left without saying goodbye.
- 아쉽다 | to be a pity, to be sorry to miss | aswipda | Regret for what ended or didn't happen: 아쉽게 졌어요 | 여행이 벌써 끝나서 너무 아쉬워요. | It's such a shame the trip is already over.
- 속상하다 | to be upset, to be heartbroken | soksanghada | 속 (inside) + 상하다 (be hurt): inner frustration | 열심히 준비했는데 떨어져서 속상해요. | I'm upset because I prepared hard and still failed.
- 답답하다 | to feel stifled, to be frustrating | dapdapada | Stuffy air, a slow situation, or a person who won't get it | 방에 창문이 없어서 좀 답답해요. | The room has no windows, so it feels a bit stuffy.
- 편안하다 | to be comfortable, to be at ease | pyeonanhada | 便安: mind at ease. 편하다 is the shorter, casual form | 집에 오니까 마음이 편안해요. | Now that I'm home, I feel at ease.
- 신나다 | to be excited, to have fun | sinnada | Upbeat, lively fun; 신나는 음악 = upbeat music | 내일 놀이공원에 가서 너무 신나요. | I'm so excited to go to the amusement park tomorrow.
- 설레다 | to be excited, to flutter | seolleda | Heart-fluttering anticipation: a date, a first trip. Not 설레이다 | 첫 해외여행이라서 마음이 설레요. | It's my first trip abroad, so my heart is fluttering.
- 감동하다 | to be moved, to be touched | gamdonghada | 感動: feelings moved. 감동적이다 = to be moving | 친구의 편지를 읽고 크게 감동했어요. | I was deeply touched after reading my friend's letter.
- 감동 | being moved, deep emotion | gamdong | 감동을 받다 (be moved) / 감동을 주다 (move someone) | 그 영화는 저에게 큰 감동을 줬어요. | That movie really moved me.
- 기대되다 | to look forward to | gidaedoeda | The event is the subject: 방학이 기대돼요. 기대하다 takes 을/를 | 다음 주 콘서트가 정말 기대돼요. | I'm really looking forward to the concert next week.
- 실망하다 | to be disappointed | silmanghada | Takes 에: 결과에 실망하다. 실망시키다 = to disappoint someone | 음식 맛이 별로라서 좀 실망했어요. | The food didn't taste great, so I was a bit disappointed.
- 만족하다 | to be satisfied | manjokada | Pronounced 만조카다. Takes 에: 결과에 만족하다 | 새로 이사한 집에 아주 만족해요. | I'm very satisfied with the house I just moved into.
- 귀찮다 | to be a hassle, to be annoying | gwichanta | Pronounced 귀찬타. Too much bother: 요리하기 귀찮아요 | 너무 피곤해서 청소하기가 귀찮아요. | I'm so tired that cleaning feels like a hassle.
- 짜증 | irritation, annoyance | jjajeung | 짜증이 나다 (get annoyed) / 짜증을 내다 (snap at someone) | 버스가 안 와서 짜증이 났어요. | The bus didn't come, so I got irritated.
- 짜증나다 | to be annoyed, to be irritated | jjajeungnada | Dictionary spacing is 짜증 나다; blunt, so avoid it with elders | 길이 막혀서 정말 짜증났어요. | The traffic was so bad that I got really annoyed.
- 미워하다 | to hate, to dislike | miwohada | Action verb, takes 을/를. 밉다 describes the feeling | 동생이 저를 미워하는 것 같아요. | I think my little brother hates me.
- 밉다 | to be hateful, to be dislikable | mipda | The disliked person is the subject: 그 사람이 미워요 | 동생이 가끔 밉지만 그래도 사랑해요. | Sometimes I can't stand my little brother, but I still love him.
- 그립다 | to miss, to long for | geuripda | The missed one is the subject: 고향이 그리워요. ㅂ→우 | 외국에 사니까 한국 음식이 그리워요. | Living abroad, I miss Korean food.
- 그리워하다 | to miss, to yearn for | geuriwohada | Adjective + 아/어하다 = verb, takes 을/를: 가족을 그리워하다 | 할머니는 돌아가신 할아버지를 그리워하세요. | My grandmother misses my late grandfather.
- 당황하다 | to be flustered, to be taken aback | danghwanghada | 唐慌: panic when something unexpected happens | 갑자기 질문을 받아서 당황했어요. | I was flustered when I was suddenly asked a question.
- 놀랍다 | to be surprising, to be amazing | nollapda | Describes the thing: 놀라운 소식. 놀라다 = I am surprised | 그 아이의 피아노 실력이 정말 놀라워요. | That child's piano skills are truly amazing.
- 두렵다 | to be afraid, to be fearful | duryeopda | More formal than 무섭다; often about the future or failure | 새로운 일을 시작하는 것이 조금 두려워요. | I'm a little afraid of starting something new.
- 안심하다 | to be relieved, to feel safe | ansimhada | 安心: peace of mind. 안심하세요 = don't worry, rest easy | 아이가 집에 무사히 와서 안심했어요. | I was relieved that my child got home safely.
- 마음 | heart, mind | maeum | 마음에 들다 = to like; 마음이 넓다 = to be generous | 그 사람은 마음이 정말 따뜻해요. | That person has a really warm heart.
- 성격 | personality, character | seonggyeok | 性格. Ask: 성격이 어때요? Answer with 밝다, 조용하다, etc. | 제 동생은 성격이 아주 밝아요. | My younger sibling has a very cheerful personality.
- 상냥하다 | to be kind, to be friendly | sangnyanghada | Gentle, sweet manner in speech: 상냥하게 말하다 | 그 가게 직원은 항상 상냥해요. | The clerk at that shop is always friendly.
- 활발하다 | to be outgoing, to be lively | hwalbalhada | 活潑: active, energetic. Opposite feel: 조용하다, 얌전하다 | 우리 반 반장은 성격이 활발해요. | Our class president has an outgoing personality.
- 밝다 | to be cheerful, to be bright | bakda | Pronounced 박따, but 밝아요 is 발가요. For light, faces and personalities | 그 친구는 늘 웃고 표정이 밝아요. | That friend always smiles and looks cheerful.
- 얌전하다 | to be well-behaved, to be quiet | yamjeonhada | Calm and polite, often of kids: 얌전하게 앉아 있다 | 아이가 식당에서 얌전하게 앉아 있었어요. | The child sat quietly and behaved well at the restaurant.
- 부지런하다 | to be diligent, to be hardworking | bujireonhada | Adverb 부지런히: 부지런히 일하다. Opposite: 게으르다 (lazy) | 아버지는 부지런해서 매일 일찍 일어나세요. | My father is hardworking and gets up early every day.
- 게으르다 | to be lazy | geeureuda | 르 irregular: 게을러요. Opposite of 부지런하다 | 저는 방학에는 좀 게으른 편이에요. | I tend to be a bit lazy during vacation.
- 성실하다 | to be sincere, to be hardworking | seongsilhada | 誠實: reliable and faithful; top praise for a student or worker | 민수 씨는 성실해서 회사에서 인기가 많아요. | Minsu is diligent and reliable, so he's well liked at work.
- 솔직하다 | to be frank, to be honest | soljikada | Pronounced 솔찌카다. 솔직히 말하면 = to be honest | 제 생각을 솔직하게 말해도 돼요? | Can I tell you what I honestly think?
- 정직하다 | to be honest, to be upright | jeongjikada | 正直: never lying or cheating; 솔직하다 = speaking your mind | 그 가게 주인은 정직해서 믿을 수 있어요. | The shop owner is honest, so you can trust him.
- 겸손하다 | to be modest, to be humble | gyeomsonhada | 謙遜. A key virtue; opposite of bragging (자랑하다) | 그 선수는 유명한데도 아주 겸손해요. | Even though that athlete is famous, he's very humble.
- 꼼꼼하다 | to be meticulous, to be careful | kkomkkomhada | Checks every detail: 꼼꼼하게 확인하다 | 언니는 꼼꼼해서 실수를 거의 안 해요. | My older sister is meticulous and rarely makes mistakes.
- 급하다 | to be impatient, to be urgent | geupada | 성격이 급하다 = impatient; 급한 일 = urgent matter | 저는 성격이 급해서 기다리는 게 힘들어요. | I'm impatient, so waiting is hard for me.
- 느긋하다 | to be relaxed, to be easygoing | neugeutada | Pronounced 느그타다. 느긋하다 (easygoing) ↔ 급하다 (impatient) | 주말 아침에는 느긋하게 커피를 마셔요. | On weekend mornings I drink coffee at a leisurely pace.
- 차분하다 | to be calm, to be composed | chabunhada | Calm voice or mood even under pressure: 차분하게 말하다 | 선생님은 항상 차분하게 설명해 주세요. | My teacher always explains things calmly.
- 씩씩하다 | to be spirited, to be brave | ssikssikada | Cheerful toughness, often praise for kids who don't cry | 우리 아들은 주사를 맞을 때도 씩씩했어요. | My son was so brave, even when he got a shot.
- 용감하다 | to be brave, to be courageous | yonggamhada | 勇敢. Bold in danger; 씩씩하다 is lighter, everyday grit | 불 속에서 아이를 구한 소방관은 정말 용감해요. | The firefighter who saved a child from the fire is truly brave.
- 똑똑하다 | to be smart, to be clever | ttokttokada | Pronounced 똑또카다. 똑똑히 = clearly: 똑똑히 기억하다 | 제 조카는 어리지만 아주 똑똑해요. | My nephew is young, but he's very smart.
- 웃기다 | to be funny, to make laugh | utgida | 웃다 (laugh) → 웃기다 (make laugh). 웃긴 영화 = a funny movie | 그 개그맨은 정말 웃겨요. | That comedian is really funny.
- 예의 | manners, courtesy | yeui | 禮儀. Pronounced 예이. 예의가 바르다 = to be polite | 어른에게는 예의를 지켜야 해요. | You should mind your manners with elders.
- 고집 | stubbornness | gojip | 固執. 고집이 세다 = to be stubborn; 고집을 부리다 = to dig in your heels | 제 동생은 고집이 세서 말을 안 들어요. | My brother is so stubborn that he won't listen.
- 욕심 | greed, desire | yoksim | 욕심이 많다 = greedy, ambitious; 욕심을 부리다 = to be greedy | 욕심을 부려서 음식을 너무 많이 샀어요. | I got greedy and bought way too much food.
- 화내다 | to lose one's temper, to get mad at | hwanaeda | Shown toward someone: 나한테 화내지 마. 화나다 = feel angry | 아빠는 제가 늦게 와서 화내셨어요. | Dad got mad at me because I came home late.
- 웃음 | laughter, smile | useum | 웃다 → 웃음. 웃음이 나오다 = can't help laughing | 교실에 아이들의 웃음소리가 가득했어요. | The classroom was full of children's laughter.
- 눈물 | tears | nunmul | 눈 (eye) + 물 (water). 눈물이 나다 = tears come | 영화가 슬퍼서 눈물이 났어요. | The movie was so sad that I cried.
- 울음 | crying, a cry | ureum | 울다 → 울음. 울음을 터뜨리다 = to burst into tears | 아기의 울음소리에 잠이 깼어요. | I woke up to the sound of the baby crying.
- 미소 | smile | miso | 微笑: a quiet smile, no sound. 미소를 짓다 = to smile | 할머니가 미소를 지으며 저를 보셨어요. | My grandmother looked at me with a smile.
- 행복 | happiness | haengbok | 幸福. Adjective: 행복하다. Wish: 행복하세요! | 가족과 함께하는 시간이 가장 큰 행복이에요. | Time with my family is my greatest happiness.
- 슬픔 | sadness, sorrow | seulpeum | 슬프다 → 슬픔 (drop 으, add 음). 기쁨 ↔ 슬픔 | 친구를 잃은 슬픔이 오래갔어요. | The sadness of losing my friend lasted a long time.
- 기쁨 | joy, happiness | gippeum | 기쁘다 → 기쁨. Paired phrase: 기쁨과 슬픔 (joys and sorrows) | 합격 소식을 듣고 기쁨의 눈물을 흘렸어요. | I cried tears of joy when I heard I'd passed.
- 즐거움 | pleasure, fun | jeulgeoum | Noun of 즐겁다: the ㅂ turns into 우, then add 음 | 여행의 즐거움은 새로운 음식을 먹는 거예요. | The fun of traveling is eating new food.
- 외로움 | loneliness | oeroum | 외롭다 → 외로움. 외로움을 타다 = to get lonely easily | 혼자 살면서 외로움을 많이 느꼈어요. | I felt very lonely while living alone.
- 부담 | burden, pressure | budam | 負擔. 부담이 되다 (feel pressure); 부담 갖지 마세요 = no pressure | 비싼 선물은 저한테 부담이 돼요. | Expensive gifts feel like a burden to me.
- 스트레스 | stress | seuteureseu | 스트레스를 받다 (get stressed) / 스트레스를 풀다 (relieve it) | 저는 운동으로 스트레스를 풀어요. | I relieve stress by working out.
- 위로하다 | to comfort, to console | wirohada | 慰勞. Noun: 위로. 위로가 되다 = to be a comfort | 시험에 떨어진 친구를 위로했어요. | I comforted my friend who failed the exam.
- 칭찬하다 | to praise, to compliment | chingchanhada | Takes 을/를: 학생을 칭찬하다. Opposite: 혼내다 (scold) | 선생님이 제 숙제를 칭찬하셨어요. | My teacher praised my homework.
- 칭찬 | praise, compliment | chingchan | 칭찬을 받다 (be praised) / 칭찬을 듣다 (hear praise) | 사장님께 칭찬을 들어서 기분이 좋아요. | I feel good because my boss praised me.
- 후회하다 | to regret | huhoehada | 後悔: after + regret. Pattern: -(으)ㄴ 것을 후회하다 | 어제 친구에게 화낸 것을 후회해요. | I regret getting angry at my friend yesterday.
- 참다 | to hold back, to endure | chamda | Hold in anger, tears, pain or a laugh: 웃음을 참다 | 너무 아팠지만 울음을 참았어요. | It hurt a lot, but I held back my tears.
- 놀리다 | to tease, to make fun of | nollida | Takes 을/를: 친구를 놀리다. Don't mix up with 놀다 (to play) | 친구들이 제 머리 모양을 놀렸어요. | My friends made fun of my hairstyle.
- 싸우다 | to fight, to argue | ssauda | Takes 와/과 or 하고: 동생하고 싸우다. Noun: 싸움 | 어렸을 때 형하고 자주 싸웠어요. | I often fought with my older brother as a kid.
- 화해하다 | to make up, to reconcile | hwahaehada | 和解. 싸우다 (fight) → 화해하다 (make up); takes 와/과 | 어제 싸운 친구와 오늘 화해했어요. | Today I made up with the friend I fought with yesterday.
- 믿다 | to believe, to trust | mitda | Pronounced 믿따. 믿을 수 있다 = trustworthy; noun 믿음 | 저는 제 친구의 말을 믿어요. | I believe what my friend says.
- 친하다 | to be close, to be friendly | chinhada | Takes 와/과 or 하고: 민수하고 친해요. 친해지다 = become close | 저는 옆집 친구와 아주 친해요. | I'm very close with my friend next door.
- 다정하다 | to be affectionate, to be warm | dajeonghada | 多情: warm and caring, esp. to family or a partner | 남편은 다정해서 늘 저를 챙겨 줘요. | My husband is affectionate and always looks after me.
- 무뚝뚝하다 | to be curt, to be unaffectionate | muttukttukada | Few words, little warmth shown. Opposite: 다정하다 | 아버지는 무뚝뚝하지만 마음은 따뜻하세요. | My father seems gruff, but he has a warm heart.
- 수줍다 | to be shy, to be bashful | sujupda | 수줍어하다 = to act shy; 수줍게 웃다 = to smile shyly | 그 아이는 수줍어서 인사를 작게 했어요. | The child was shy and said hello quietly.
